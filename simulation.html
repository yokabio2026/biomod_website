Simulation

Purpose

ALiGN aims to build a network in which GUVs connect to selected partners and exchange their contents through those connections. Understanding how DNA-mediated connections influence GUV aggregation is an important step toward controlling the formation of this network.

Our simulation examines how DNA surface density and DNA bond lifetime affect GUV aggregation. We model GUV diffusion, DNA-mediated binding, and dissociation, and track the numbers of monomers, dimers, and larger clusters over time. By comparing these outcomes across conditions, we investigate how the two parameters influence the extent and time course of aggregation.

Model

This model simulates the motion and aggregation of two types of equally sized GUVs in three dimensions. It combines Brownian motion, DNA-mediated binding and dissociation, and the recruitment of membrane-bound DNA into contact regions. The numbers of monomers, dimers and larger clusters are tracked over time to examine how DNA surface density and DNA bond lifetime affect aggregation.

The flowchart below summarizes the simulation procedure, followed by a brief explanation of each step.

GUV Simulation Workflow

Simulation steps and equations

  1. Read the settings. Load the simulation parameters and command-line arguments. DNA bond lifetime and surface density are input parameters.
  2. Initialize the system. Select a DNA surface density and initialize the GUV positions, DNA populations, bond states and time.
    Ntotal,i=4πR2ρi
    Di=kBT6πηR

    R is the common GUV radius, ρi is its DNA surface density, T is temperature, and η is solvent viscosity. The diffusion coefficient is calculated in SI units and converted to µm²/s. Initially, all DNA is free and no GUV pairs are bonded.

  3. Calculate geometry and timing. Calculate separations using the minimum-image convention and approximate the contact area for equal-radius spheres.
    dij=xi−xj−Lnint((xi−xj)L)
    rij=‖dij‖,Sij=πRmax(2R+dl−rij,0)
    Δt=min(Δtmax,Δtforce,Δtcontact,Δtprob,tnext−t)

    L is the box length, dl is the DNA reach, and nint rounds each coordinate to the nearest integer. The adaptive time step obeys the implemented force-stability, contact-resolution and probability limits, and stops at the next recording time. If the required step is below the configured minimum, the calculation stops.

  4. Update DNA-mediated connections. Apply the following three operations in order. Free DNA populations used in each operation reflect the preceding updates.

    4.1 Dissociation of existing pairs

    Poff,ij=[1−e−Δtτoff]Bij

    A bonded pair dissociates when a uniform random number u ∈ [0, 1) is smaller than this probability. Here Bij = qij is the effective bridge count, which can be non-integer. Dissociation removes the entire pair connection and returns its allocated DNA to both GUVs; individual partial breakage is not tracked.

    Nfree,i←Nfree,i+qij,Nfree,j←Nfree,j+qij,qij←0

    The DNA bond lifetime τoff is specified directly as an input parameter in seconds.

    4.2 DNA recruitment into surviving contacts

    mij=max(0,min(Nfree,i,Nfree,j,SijaDNA−qij))
    τl,ij=log10[11.55(Sij2πR2)−2.26]R2Dl
    gij=mij(1−e−Δtτl,ij)
    si=min(1,Nfree,i∑kgik),Δqij=gijmin(si,sj)
    qij←qij+Δqij,Nfree,i←Nfree,i−Δqij,Nfree,j←Nfree,j−Δqij

    aDNA is the footprint per linker and Dl is the membrane diffusion coefficient. Recruitment is evaluated only for surviving contacts with positive area. The scaling factor is 1 when there are no requests. After recruitment, Bij is set to qij. Shrinking contact area limits further recruitment but does not remove existing bridges.

    4.3 Formation of new bonds

    Pon,ij=min(Nfree,iSij4πR2,Nfree,jSij4πR2)(1−e−Δtτh)

    For an eligible unbound A–B pair, a new bridge forms if a fresh uniform random number u ∈ [0, 1) is smaller than Pon,ij. Each GUV must have at least one free linker, and the contact capacity must be at least one. A pair that dissociated in this step cannot rebind in the same step. τh is the hybridization time parameter; probabilities are not clipped.

    qij=Bij=1,Nfree,i←Nfree,i−1,Nfree,j←Nfree,j−1
  5. Move the GUVs. Calculate repulsive and bond forces, update the GUV positions, and apply periodic boundaries.
    fWCAij=24ε*rij2[2(σrij)12−(σrij)6]dij
    fFENEij=−κ1−(rijrmax)2dij
    σ=2R216,κ=kfactorR2,rmax=2Rfmax

    Forces are expressed as f = F/(kBT). The WCA term applies for rij < 2R and is zero otherwise. The FENE term applies only to bonded pairs and is not multiplied by the bridge count. ε* is epsilon_kbt, kfactor is fene_stiffness_factor, and fmax is fene_max_distance_factor. Their defaults are 10, 7.5 and 1.5, respectively. The total fi is the sum of pair forces.

    x′i=xi+DifiΔt+2DiΔtξi
    xi←x′i−L⌊x′iL⌋,t←t+Δt

    Each component of ξi is an independent standard normal random variable. Coordinates, diffusion coefficients and time are used consistently in µm, µm²/s and s. Periodic wrapping is applied component by component.

  6. Count clusters. At each recording time, classify the current connected components.
    C1=∑c ∈ 𝒞𝟙[|c|=1],C2=∑c ∈ 𝒞𝟙[|c|=2],C3+=∑c ∈ 𝒞𝟙[|c|≥3]

    𝒞 is the set of connected components formed by the currently bonded GUV pairs, including isolated GUVs; |c| is the number of GUVs in a component. Each dimer or larger cluster counts as one cluster. No equilibrium calculation or waiting period is required for this classification.

  7. Repeat the calculation. Repeat steps 3–6 until the specified end time is reached. The time step is recomputed as the configuration changes.
  8. Save the results. Save the simulation results as CSV files and plots.
  9. Continue or finish. Repeat the simulation for the next DNA surface density, or finish when all conditions have been completed.
