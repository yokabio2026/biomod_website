Design

What is the ALiGN

ALiGN is a reproduction of a cell-to-cell network using GUVs. In this project, we aim to realize a network of three GUVs (GUV1, GUV2 and GUV3). Connecting three GUVs requires two different connections, GUV1–GUV2 and GUV2–GUV3, which must not interfere with each other. If these two connections can be designed independently, the same idea can be extended to three, four or more connections, and to larger networks of GUVs.

The four elements required to reproduce a cell-to-cell network are achieved as follows.

Connection (see Specific Connection Between GUVs)
1. Connecting with intended partners: DNA strands with different sequences on the GUV surface let each GUV connect only to its intended partner (sequence-specific binding).
2. Connecting with an intended number of partners: very short DNA strands with weak binding, together with a tuned DNA surface density, prevent aggregation so that each GUV connects only to the intended number of partners (weak binding and surface density control).

Material exchange (see Pore Formation for Material Exchange)
3. Exchanging contents between connected GUVs: α-hemolysin forms pores in the GUV membrane, opening a path for contents to move between connected GUVs.
4. Preventing leakage from unconnected GUVs: TRIMEB acts as a lid on the pores, which prevents the contents of GUVs that are not connected from leaking out.

Specific Connection Between GUVs

In ALiGN, connections between GUVs are controlled in two ways: connecting with intended partners (sequence-specific binding) and connecting with an intended number of partners (weak binding and surface density control).

Sequence-Specific Binding

To connect only the intended partners, we place single-stranded DNA on the surface of each GUV and use the complementarity of DNA. A GUV can bind only to a GUV that carries the complementary strand.

We prepare two DNA pairs with different sequences: one for the GUV1–GUV2 connection and one for the GUV2–GUV3 connection. Because the sequences are different, each pair recognizes only its own partner, and the two connections do not interfere with each other. As a result, GUV1 connects to GUV2 and GUV2 connects to GUV3, but GUV1 does not connect to GUV3.

Weak Binding and Surface Density Control

Weak Binding

Connecting with intended partners is not enough to control the number of connections. Even with specific DNA, a GUV could connect to many partners of the right type at the same time, and GUVs could form large aggregates. In the study of Hadorn et al., GUVs carrying complementary DNA formed assemblies that were visible by eye [1]. If only a few DNA strands were enough to keep a connection, GUVs would keep aggregating.

Therefore, we use very short 6-nt DNA strands and design each duplex to be very weak. Only 4 of the 6 bases actually pair; the remaining two bases, TT, sit next to the membrane as a spacer. Using sticky ends as short as four bases has been proposed for micrometer-sized DNA-coated particles as a way to avoid aggregation [2]. A single duplex cannot hold two GUVs together; a connection holds only when many strands bind at the same time.

Surface Density Control

Once a GUV connects to its intended partner, most of the strands on the facing surfaces are used in that connection. The few strands left over are not enough to hold an extra partner, so the GUV does not connect any further. The key parameter is therefore the surface density of DNA on each GUV. By tuning it, we aim to obtain many sets of exactly three GUVs connected as GUV1–GUV2–GUV3.

Pore Formation for Material Exchange

Controlling the connections between GUVs alone does not satisfy the four elements that ALiGN requires. Connected GUVs must also be able to exchange contents with each other, like cells that communicate. To achieve this, two conditions must be met at the same time: connected GUVs can exchange their contents, and GUVs that are not connected do not leak their contents to the outside. Exchanging contents between connected GUVs is achieved with α-hemolysin, and preventing leakage from unconnected GUVs is achieved with TRIMEB.

α-Hemolysin as a Pore

α-Hemolysin is a pore-forming protein from Staphylococcus aureus. Its seven subunits assemble in a lipid membrane and form a pore that is 1.4 nm wide at its narrowest point and lets small molecules pass through [3]. Engineered α-hemolysin added to the outside of GUVs forms pores in the membrane and lets an encapsulated dye leak out [4]. By forming α-hemolysin pores in the GUV membrane, we open a path for the contents of a GUV to move to its partner GUV.

TRIMEB as a Lid

An open pore alone would let the contents leak out through the surface of a GUV even when the GUV is not connected to any partner. To prevent this, we use TRIMEB (heptakis(2,3,6-tri-O-methyl)-β-cyclodextrin), a derivative of β-cyclodextrin that binds reversibly in the lumen of the α-hemolysin pore and blocks it like a lid [3]. The more TRIMEB is present, the more slowly the contents leak through the pore [3].

While a GUV is floating alone, the lid prevents its contents from leaking out. When two GUVs are connected and their membranes come close to each other, the two GUVs become able to exchange contents [5]. In this way, material exchange takes place mainly between connected GUVs.

DNA Sequence Design

These are the DNA strands used in ALiGN. Each strand that sits on a GUV carries a thiol (SH) modification, which anchors it to the GUV membrane.

6-nt DNA (Main Design)

These are weak-binding strands for connecting GUVs with an intended number of partners (see Weak Binding and Surface Density Control). Each strand has a 4-nt binding part (ACAC / GTGT or AGAG / CTCT) and a TT spacer on the membrane side. All strands carry SH at the 3′ end.

Pair 1 (GUV1 – GUV2)
Conn6_L1: 5′-ACACTT-3′ (SH)
Conn6_R1: 5′-GTGTTT-3′ (SH)

Pair 2 (GUV2 – GUV3)
Conn6_L2: 5′-AGAGTT-3′ (SH)
Conn6_R2: 5′-CTCTTT-3′ (SH)

23-bp DNA (Supporting Design)

These are strongly binding strands that form a stable 23-bp duplex. We use them first to show that DNA can control the GUV1–GUV2 and GUV2–GUV3 connections separately (see Sequence-Specific Binding). SH is at the 3′ end on the L side and at the 5′ end on the R side.

The 23-nt sequences themselves are taken from a published set of orthonormal 23-mers [6]. Every sequence in that set binds only to its own complement and not to the other sequences in the set, so the GUV1–GUV2 pair and the GUV2–GUV3 pair do not interfere with each other.

We make these strands long because a 23-bp duplex is strong and hardly comes apart, so only a few duplexes are enough to hold two GUVs together. With such strong binding, a GUV keeps taking new partners, so the number of partners is not controlled. When we use the 23-bp DNA, we do not aim to control the number of partners; we only use it to show that the sequence can control the choice of partners, that is, the GUV1–GUV2 and GUV2–GUV3 connections.

The way the two strands are joined also matters. A duplex is about 0.34 nm long per base pair, so a 23-bp duplex standing between the two membranes would hold them about 8 nm apart, which makes the exchange of contents through α-hemolysin harder. We therefore anchor one strand at its 3′ end and the other at its 5′ end, so that the two strands pair side by side starting from the point where the membranes touch, and the duplex extends outwards along the membranes instead of standing between them. The two membranes can then come close to each other.

Pair 1 (GUV1 – GUV2)
Conn23_L1: 5′-GCATCTACACTCAATACCCAGCC-3′ (SH)
Conn23_R1: (SH) 5′-GGCTGGGTATTGAGTGTAGATGC-3′

Pair 2 (GUV2 – GUV3)
Conn23_L2: 5′-CTAGTCCATTGTAACGAAGGCCA-3′ (SH)
Conn23_R2: (SH) 5′-TGGCCTTCGTTACAATGGACTAG-3′

63-nt Scaffold DNA (Backup Plan)

The 63-nt scaffold DNA is a backup plan in case the weak 6-nt binding alone rarely starts a connection. The 63-nt scaffold DNA has a 23-nt end that hybridizes to the 23-bp anchor strand on one GUV, and the remaining 40 nt is a repeat (CA or CT, ten 4-nt units) that gathers many 6-nt strands of the partner GUV at one spot, creating a trigger for connection.

Conn6_L1X: 5′-GGCTGGGTATTGAGTGTAGATGCCACACACACACACACACACACACACACACACACACACACA-3′
(binds Conn23_L1 on GUV1; the CA repeat gathers Conn6_R1 on GUV2)

Conn6_R2X: 5′-CTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTCTAGTCCATTGTAACGAAGGCCA-3′
(binds Conn23_R2 on GUV3; the CT repeat gathers Conn6_L2 on GUV2)
